I once stood in a Restoration Hardware showroom, picked up a price tag, and audibly said 'absolutely not' to a stranger's toddler. That plant stand was $895. I found something 90% as good at HomeGoods for $34.99 two weeks later. That's the whole game with high low decor dupes: you're not settling, you're just refusing to pay for a brand name stamped on the bottom of a lamp.

The trick is knowing which pieces are worth the splurge and which ones nobody can tell apart from six feet away. Spoiler: almost none of them can be told apart. Here are fifteen swaps that have fooled every single person who's sat on my couch.

## The Living Room Swaps

The CB2 Peekaboo coffee table runs about $599 and looks like a sculpture. Target's Threshold acrylic-base tables and various Amazon dupes get you the same floating, see-through effect for under $150. Nobody is crawling around your coffee table checking for a maker's mark.

Serena and Lily's rattan and wicker pieces are gorgeous and genuinely well made, but a $700 lounge chair is a big ask for most of us. World Market and Target both carry rattan chairs in the $200 to $300 range that have the same warm, textural look. The weave isn't quite as tight, but from across the room, it reads identical.

The Anthropologie Turned Wood Floor Lamp is a Pinterest favorite at $498. I've seen near-identical turned wood lamps at HomeGoods for $89 and on Amazon for even less. Wood tone and lampshade shape do 95% of the visual work here, not the brand.

## The Bedroom Swaps

A linen duvet from a brand like Cultiver or Parachute can run $300 or more. Target's Threshold linen blend and Amazon's Bedsure linen sets get you that rumpled, lived-in texture for $60 to $90. Wash it in cold water and let it air dry a little wrinkled. That's honestly half the look anyway.

Boucle headboards are everywhere right now, and the designer versions from places like Interior Define start around $1,200. Amazon and Wayfair both sell boucle headboards in the $150 to $250 range, and the fabric feels nearly the same to the touch. I own one. My mother-in-law asked where I got it custom made.

Ceramic table lamps with a fluted or ribbed base, the kind you see styled in every West Elm catalog photo, run $150 to $200 there. HomeGoods stocks near-identical versions for $30 to $50 on a regular basis. This is the single easiest dupe hunt on this whole list.

## The Dining Room Swaps

Rattan pendant lights from Serena and Lily or Anthropologie can hit $400 to $600. Amazon has dozens of rattan and paper lantern pendants under $100 that give you the same dappled, warm light and woven texture. I'd spend real money on a dining table before I'd spend it here.

A plaster-look pendant or sconce, the organic lumpy kind that looks hand-sculpted, sells for hundreds at boutique lighting shops. Target's Threshold and Hearth and Hand lines have released plaster-finish lighting for $40 to $70 more than once. Check their lighting section every few months because it rotates.

Woven dining chairs with a wishbone or Danish silhouette can cost $300 a chair at retailers like Article. Target, Walmart, and Amazon all sell similar woven and rattan-back dining chairs for $80 to $120 each. Buy four and you've saved close to $800.

## The Wall and Textile Swaps

Vintage-style Turkish or Moroccan rugs from The Citizenry or Boutique Rugs start at $400 for a small size and climb fast. Ruggable and Lulu and Georgia both make machine-washable versions with a similar faded, worn-in pattern for $150 to $250. Mine has survived two dogs and a red wine incident.

Original art is wonderful if you can afford it, but a lot of what's framed in magazine-worthy rooms is actually a print. Etsy sellers offer high-resolution digital downloads of vintage botanical, abstract, and landscape art for $5 to $15 that you print at home or at Walmart Photo. Frame it well and nobody knows the difference.

A jute or sisal-look rug in a large size can run $500 to $800 from higher-end retailers. Target's Threshold jute rugs and various Amazon dupes cover an 8x10 space for $150 to $250. The texture is what sells it, and both versions have plenty of it.

## The Small Stuff That Matters More Than You Think

Weighted, sculptural ceramic vases in that lumpy organic shape everyone's obsessed with can cost $80 to $150 individually at boutique shops. HomeGoods and Target both carry versions for $15 to $25, often in the exact same oatmeal and terracotta tones.

Brass or unlacquered-brass hardware and accents, like drawer pulls or picture frame ledges, look expensive because brass reads as expensive. Amazon sells brass-finish pulls in bulk for a few dollars each, and they patina in a similar way over time.

Linen or cotton throw pillow covers from designer home shops can run $40 to $60 each, which adds up fast if you need six. IKEA and Target sell nearly identical textured covers for $10 to $15, and swapping just the cover instead of the whole pillow saves you money twice.

## Why This Actually Works

High low decor dupes work because most of what reads as 'expensive' in a room is texture, shape, and color, not the label sewn into the seam. A rattan chair looks rattan whether it cost $200 or $900. Once you start shopping by silhouette and material instead of by brand name, the whole game opens up. Your wallet will thank you, and so will your Pinterest board.

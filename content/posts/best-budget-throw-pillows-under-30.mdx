Throw pillows are the fastest, cheapest way to change how a room feels, and also the easiest way
to waste money on something that looks flat and lifeless within a month. A pillow cover is only
half the equation. The insert underneath it matters just as much.

I've bought a lot of bad ones to get to this list. Here's what's actually held up.

## What makes a throw pillow worth buying

Two things separate a good budget pillow from a bad one: an insert that's genuinely full,
usually down-alternative or a poly-fill blend rated for the cover size, and a cover with a zipper
closure instead of a sewn envelope back. A zipper means you can wash it. An envelope back usually
means the fill shifts and lumps within weeks.

## What to skip

Skip any pillow that only lists "polyester" with no fill weight or density mentioned anywhere in
the listing. That's usually a sign of thin, cheap stuffing that'll flatten within a few weeks,
regardless of how good the cover fabric looks in photos.

A $20 pillow that still looks full in six months beats a $12 one you're refluffing every single
day. That's the whole equation.

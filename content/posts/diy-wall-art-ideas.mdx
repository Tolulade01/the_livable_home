You furnished the room. You bought the rug, the throw pillows, maybe even a plant you'll keep alive for three weeks. And then you looked up and realized the walls are still completely bare. That's usually where people stall out, because art feels expensive and personal in a way a coffee table doesn't. But DIY wall art ideas exist for exactly this problem, and most of them cost less than a single framed print from a big box store.

I've made probably a dozen of these for my own place, and the secret nobody tells you is that art doesn't have to be good, it just has to be intentional. A big shape in a color you love, hung at the right height, reads as designed. Here are 18 ways to fill that wall this weekend, roughly organized from stupid-easy to slightly more involved.

## Low-Effort Ideas You Can Finish In An Hour

Start here if you want instant results without buying much.

A single dried palm frond or eucalyptus stem pinned to the wall costs nothing if you already have a plant, and it reads surprisingly sculptural against a plain wall. Buy a $6 stem at Trader Joe's if you don't.

Tape off a simple shape, like an arch or a soft blob, with painter's tape and fill it in with leftover wall paint in a contrasting shade. This is basically free if you have paint samples sitting in a drawer.

Frame a piece of pretty wrapping paper or wallpaper scrap. HomeGoods sells frames for $8 to $15, and a sheet of nice paper from an art store runs about $5.

Hang a woven basket or plate on its own as a wall object. This isn't technically art, but it fills space the same way and costs whatever the thrift store charges, usually $4 to $10.

Pin up a large piece of fabric, like a scarf or a remnant from the fabric store, using a wooden dowel top and bottom. A yard of linen is around $10 and looks like a tapestry once it's hung.

## Ideas That Use What You Already Have

These lean on stuff sitting in a closet or junk drawer right now.

Old book pages, sheet music, or vintage postcards look intentional in a grid of small $3 frames from Ikea. Five or six of them together make a real statement without much effort.

A collection of postcards from trips you've taken, pinned directly to the wall in a loose cluster, tells a better story than most store-bought art anyway.

Kids' artwork, matted in a plain frame instead of taped to the fridge, instantly looks like a gallery piece. This one's free and it will make you cry a little, in a good way.

Press flowers from a bouquet you already bought, dry them for a week between book pages, then frame them under glass. It takes patience more than money.

An old scarf, tie, or piece of fabric with sentimental value, stretched over a cheap canvas frame from Michaels ($4 for an 8x10), becomes a piece with actual meaning behind it.

## Ideas That Take A Little More Effort But Still A Weekend

These need a trip to the craft store and maybe two hours of actual work.

Abstract paint pour art on canvas is genuinely hard to mess up. A $12 canvas and a few bottles of $4 acrylic paint from Michaels will get you a piece that looks like it belongs in a hotel lobby.

A large-scale line drawing, done freehand in black paint pen on white canvas, looks intentional even if your art skills stopped in fourth grade. Simplicity is the whole trick here.

Sponge-paint or stamp a repeating shape, like an arch or circle, across a canvas in a muted palette for something that reads as a real textile pattern.

Macrame wall hangings look complicated but are mostly just knots repeated in a pattern. A beginner kit from Amazon runs about $18 and comes with instructions.

A fabric-wrapped canvas, where you literally staple a pretty fabric remnant to the back of a canvas frame, takes fifteen minutes and looks like a designer textile piece.

Clay or plaster wall discs, poured into a silicone mold and hung with jute string once dry, have that expensive-looking organic texture everyone's chasing right now.

A typography piece, either hand-lettered or printed from a free template online and framed, works well over a bed or reading chair.

My personal favorite: cut mat board into an odd shape (an arch, a wave, a soft organic blob), paint it a single saturated color, and hang it flat against the wall with no frame at all. It costs about $6 in mat board and paint, and it looks like it came from a design shop.

## How To Actually Hang This Stuff So It Looks Right

Here's where a lot of good DIY wall art ideas fall apart: the hanging. Center art at roughly 57 to 60 inches from the floor to the middle of the piece, which is standard gallery height. Over furniture, leave 6 to 8 inches between the top of the couch or dresser and the bottom of the art.

If you're doing a cluster or gallery arrangement, lay it out on the floor first, take a photo, then adjust before you put a single nail in the wall. Command strips or small nails work fine for lightweight canvas and paper pieces, so renters don't need to stress about damage deposits.

One last thing: don't feel like you need to fill every inch of wall at once. A single oversized piece, even something as simple as the mat board cutout, often has more impact than five small pieces crammed together. Start with one wall, live with it for a week, and see what the room actually asks for next.

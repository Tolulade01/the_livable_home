Bathrooms are the room landlords redo the least and renters complain about the most. Builder-grade everything, a mirror bolted straight to the wall, lighting that makes you look faintly ill. But renter-friendly bathroom upgrades exist, and most of them cost less than a nice dinner out. None of these require a drill, a landlord's blessing, or a security deposit you'll never see again.

I've lived in four rentals in the last decade, and the bathroom always gets fixed last because it feels the most permanent. It's not. Almost everything in there is swappable, coverable, or removable with a hair dryer and some patience. Here's where I'd actually spend money first.

## Swap the Shower Curtain and Liner Immediately

This is the fastest fix in the whole list and it's not close. A thin, wrinkled shower curtain reads cheap even in a gorgeous bathroom, and a good one reads expensive even in a mediocre one. Look for a heavier cotton or linen-blend curtain, something like Target's Threshold linen shower curtain around 28 dollars, and pair it with a clear liner so you're not hiding the pattern. Skip the plastic hooks that came with your last apartment. Round metal rings, roughly 12 dollars for a set of twelve, make more difference than people expect.

## Add a Wood or Woven Bath Mat

The bath mat situation in most rentals is a sad gray bathmat that came from nowhere and belongs to no one. Replace it with a slatted teak bath mat, or go with a chunky woven cotton one if you want something softer underfoot. Either option instantly makes the floor look considered instead of accidental. Nothing to install. Just step over the old one on trash day.

## Reface Cabinet Hardware With Adhesive or Slide-On Options

Old brass or scratched builder hardware is one of those things you stop seeing until a guest points it out. You don't need to drill new holes to fix it. Cover plates and slide-on knob covers exist specifically for renters, and brands like Kwikset and various Etsy sellers make versions that snap over your existing hardware without tools. If your current knobs are a standard size, some adhesive-backed cabinet pulls will also just stick right over them. Peel it all off when you move, no patch job needed.

## Bring in a Leaning Floor Mirror

Rental bathroom mirrors are usually one flat rectangle bolted above the sink, and there's nothing to be done about that particular mirror. So don't touch it. Instead, lean a full-length mirror against an empty wall or in a corner if you have the floor space. It adds light, makes the room feel larger, and gives you an actual outfit check, which the medicine cabinet mirror never could.

## Upgrade the Showerhead Yourself

This one surprises people, but a showerhead is considered a fixture you're allowed to swap as long as you keep the original and reinstall it before move-out. A pressure-boosting showerhead, something like the Moen Attract for around 40 dollars, takes ten minutes with a wrench and no plumber. Keep the old one in a labeled bag in your closet. Future you will thank present you.

## Use Removable Wallpaper on One Wall

If your lease allows it, and a lot of leases actually do allow peel-and-stick wallpaper now, this is the single biggest visual change you can make in a bathroom. Companies like Tempaper and Spoonflower sell removable designs specifically tested for humidity. Do one accent wall, not all four, both for your sanity during application and for your security deposit. Test a small patch first and leave it up for a few days to confirm it comes off clean.

## Replace the Toilet Seat

It sounds small and it is small, but a scratched or yellowing toilet seat drags down an otherwise nice bathroom fast. A new one runs about 25 to 40 dollars and takes fifteen minutes with a screwdriver. Keep the old seat in storage. This is one of those renter-friendly bathroom upgrades nobody talks about because it's slightly unglamorous, but it works.

## Add Battery-Operated or Plug-In Lighting

Rental bathroom lighting tends to be one harsh overhead fixture that makes everyone look tired. Battery-operated LED puck lights stuck along the top of the mirror frame, or a plug-in sconce that clips onto an existing outlet, both add warmth without any wiring. Look at options from Kohree or Hyperikon under 20 dollars for the puck lights. It's a small thing that changes how the whole room feels at night.

## Bring in Real Texture With Towels

The towels that come with most first apartments are thin, mismatched, and a little sad. Upgrade to a set of Turkish cotton towels in one or two colors, something like Parachute or even Target's Casaluna line, and hang them so they actually show. It's one of the cheapest upgrades on this list, maybe 40 to 60 dollars for a full set, and it upgrades every photo you take in that room.

## Add Storage That Doesn't Touch the Wall

Rentals rarely have enough bathroom storage, and drilling in shelving is usually off the table anyway. A slim ladder shelf that leans against the wall, or a rolling cart from IKEA's Raskog line around 40 dollars, solves the problem without a single screw. Bonus: both come with you when you move, unlike built-in shelving you'd have to leave behind.

## Style the Open Surfaces Like You Mean It

This last one costs nothing but attention. Group your everyday products onto a small tray instead of letting them sprawl across the counter. Add one plant that can handle steam, like a pothos or a snake plant, and swap your soap dispenser for something ceramic instead of the plastic bottle it came in. It's the styling equivalent of making your bed. Small effort, disproportionately large payoff.

None of these renter-friendly bathroom upgrades require touching drywall, calling your landlord, or spending more than you'd spend on a nice pair of jeans. Start with the shower curtain and the towels since they're the cheapest and most visible changes you can make this weekend. The rest can happen slowly, one paycheck at a time, and your deposit will still come back.

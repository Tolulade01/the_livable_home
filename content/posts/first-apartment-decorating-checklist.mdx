You moved in three weeks ago and your living room still has a folding chair in it. Somewhere on Pinterest there's a first apartment decorating checklist promising you'll have it all figured out by move-in day, but that's not how this actually goes. It happens in waves, usually paycheck by paycheck, and that's completely normal.

What nobody tells you is that the checklist you actually need has less to do with throw pillows and more to do with sequencing. Buy things in the wrong order and you'll end up with a $40 candle and no lamp. Let's fix that.

## What To Buy Before You Buy Anything Decorative

Before you even think about art or accent pillows, you need the boring stuff that makes a space livable. That means a shower curtain and liner (the Target ones run about $12 each), a trash can for every room that generates trash, and actual light bulbs in a warm tone, not the blue-white ones builders love.

Lighting is the one people skip and regret. Overhead lighting in most rentals is harsh and unflattering, so a $25 floor lamp from Target or a couple of plug-in sconces will do more for the mood of your first apartment decorating checklist than anything else on it. Get this part right and everything you add later looks better by default.

You also need a trash can with a lid in the kitchen, over-the-door hooks for coats, and a bath mat. I know none of this is fun. But living without it for two months is worse than buying it now.

### The Furniture Order That Actually Makes Sense

Buy your bed first. Not a mattress on the floor, an actual frame, even if it's the $150 Zinus one from Amazon. Sleeping well affects everything else about how you feel in your space.

After that, prioritize seating over a coffee table, and a dresser over a nightstand. You can put a stack of books next to your bed as a nightstand for a while and it'll be fine. You cannot fold your laundry onto the floor forever without losing your mind a little.

## The Stuff That Feels Optional But Isn't

Curtains are not optional, and I will die on this hill. A bare window makes a room feel unfinished no matter what else you've done, and blackout curtains from IKEA start around $30 a panel. If your rental has ugly mini blinds, curtain rods that mount with tension (no drilling needed) are around $20 and solve the whole problem.

A bath towel set that isn't the free ones from your childhood home also counts. So does a real trash bag supply, because running out at 10pm is its own specific kind of misery. And get a doormat. It's four dollars of dignity.

### Command Strips Are Your Best Friend, Not An Afterthought

If you're renting, assume you cannot drill anything until you've checked your lease twice. Command strips and hooks hold mirrors, art, and even small shelves depending on the weight rating, and they come off without damage.

Buy the picture-hanging strips in bulk before you need them, not one pack at a time. You will use more than you think, especially once you start hanging things gallery-wall style.

## What To Skip When You're Just Starting Out

You do not need a full dining set in month one. A folding table with a tablecloth over it looks intentional, and nobody will know the difference at a dinner party. Skip matching furniture sets entirely, they read as showroom, not lived-in.

Also skip anything trendy that you're only buying because it's everywhere on your feed right now. A first apartment decorating checklist should be about function first, personality second. The Pinterest-perfect stuff can come later once you've lived in the space long enough to know what you actually want.

## The Order I'd Actually Do It In

Start with lighting and window coverings, because they change the whole feel of a room for under $100 combined. Then handle the boring functional stuff: trash cans, bath mat, hooks, hangers. After that, get real furniture piece by piece instead of all at once.

Save the fun part, the art, the throw pillows, the plants, for last. Not because it doesn't matter, but because it means so much more once the bones of the apartment actually work. That's the real first apartment decorating checklist: function, then comfort, then joy, in that order.

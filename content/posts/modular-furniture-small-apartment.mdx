My first apartment had a living room so small that my couch touched three walls at once. I didn't need more furniture, I needed furniture that could do more than one job. That's the whole case for modular furniture in a small apartment: it moves when your life moves, instead of sitting there being one fixed, unchangeable shape forever.

Modular just means the piece is made of parts you can rearrange, add to, or split up. A sectional with pieces that detach. A coffee table that splits into two stools. Furniture that adapts to movie night, a dinner party, or a random Tuesday when you just need to vacuum under the couch. Here are nine pieces worth your money, with real prices attached so you know what you're actually signing up for.

### 1. Burrow Nomad Sectional (from around $1,895 for a 3-seat configuration)

This is the gold standard of modular sofas for a reason. Each section connects with a simple peg system, no tools required, so you can reconfigure it from a straight sofa to an L-shape in about ten minutes. It also ships in boxes that fit through a normal apartment door, which sounds small until you've tried to get a regular sofa up a narrow stairwell.

### 2. IKEA Kivik Modular Sofa (from about $1,000 for a 3-seat sofa)

Cheaper than Burrow and just as flexible. You buy it in sections, so you can start with a loveseat and add a chaise later when your budget or your apartment allows. It's not going to win design awards, but it's comfortable and it holds up.

### 3. Nesting Coffee Tables, Target Threshold Line (set of 2, around $130)

One big table becomes two smaller ones the second you need floor space for yoga, a card game, or a friend who's sleeping over on an air mattress. Nesting tables are the unsung heroes of small-space furniture and nobody talks about them enough.

### 4. Poufs That Double as Ottomans and Extra Seating (World Market, around $70 each)

Buy two or three. Stack them, line them up as a bench, or scatter them for extra seating when six people show up for a party your apartment was not built for. They're light enough to move with one hand, which matters more than it sounds like it should.

### 5. The Floyd Modular Shelving System (starts around $455 for a single unit)

Unlike a built-in bookshelf, Floyd's shelves can be reconfigured into different heights and widths, and they're designed to be taken apart and moved without falling apart in the process. For renters who move every year or two, this is the shelving equivalent of a good travel bag.

### 6. Storage Ottoman With a Removable Tray Top (Target or Wayfair, around $90)

It's a coffee table. It's a footrest. It's where you hide the blankets, the extra remote, and the mail you haven't opened yet. Look for one with a tray top that lifts off completely rather than one that just flips, since the flip kind tends to spill your drink.

### 7. CB2 Stax Modular Bookcase (around $300 per cube unit)

These stack vertically or side by side, so the same three cubes can be a tall narrow bookshelf in one apartment and a low wide credenza in the next. That's the whole point of buying modular: the furniture outlives the specific room you bought it for.

### 8. A Murphy-Style Wall Bed Panel (Wayfair, starting around $600 for a twin panel)

This one's a bigger investment, but if you're working with a studio, it can genuinely double your usable floor space during the day. Not every rental allows wall-mounted installs, so check with your landlord before you get attached to the idea.

### 9. Modular Sofa-in-a-Box, Article's Sven Sectional (from around $2,000 for a corner sectional)

Sven's individual pieces can be bought separately and rearranged as your space changes, whether that's splitting the sectional into a loveseat and a chair, or reconfiguring the corner for a new layout. It's an investment piece, but it's built to be lived with for years, not just one lease.

## How to Actually Choose What You Need

Don't buy all nine of these. Start with whatever solves your biggest daily annoyance, whether that's a lack of seating, a lack of storage, or a coffee table that's in everyone's way during game night. Modular furniture works best when you buy it in pieces over time, adding what you need instead of guessing at a whole room upfront.

Measure your doorways and stairwells before anything else. A modular sofa that ships in boxes only helps if the boxes actually fit through your entryway, and I say this as someone who once had to send a couch back because of a stairwell turn nobody warned me about.

If you rent and expect to move again soon, prioritize the pieces on this list that break down without tools: the Burrow sectional, the Floyd shelving, the nesting tables. Furniture that disassembles easily is furniture you'll actually use again in the next place, instead of leaving it on the curb because it's too much of a hassle to move.

My first apartment bathroom had exactly one shelf under the sink and a medicine cabinet the size of a paperback book. If you're hunting for small bathroom storage ideas because your rental gave you a sink, a toilet, and basically nothing else, I've been there. The good news is most of the best solutions do not require drilling, painting, or a single conversation with your landlord.

Here are 13 that actually work, ranked from easiest to slightly-more-effort.

## Over-the-Toilet Storage That Does Double Duty

An over-the-toilet etagere is the single biggest bang-for-buck move in a small bathroom. The SoBuy bathroom shelf from Amazon runs about $50 and gives you two or three shelves plus sometimes a cabinet, all without touching the wall. Use the top shelf for rolled towels, the middle for baskets, and skip the bottom shelf for anything you actually need daily since it's the hardest to reach.

## Tension Rod Shelving Inside the Cabinet

If you have a cabinet under the sink, a $10 tension rod turned sideways creates an instant shelf for spray bottles to hang from by their trigger handles. It sounds like a hack because it is one, but it doubles your usable space in a cabinet that's otherwise just a pile of stuff on the floor.

## Command Hook Everything

Command hooks are the MVP of rental bathroom storage. Hang one behind the door for a robe, a few inside the cabinet door for spray bottles, and one near the shower for a loofah so it's not sitting in a puddle on the tub ledge. They hold surprisingly well and come off without paint damage, which is the whole point.

## A Shower Caddy That Hangs, Not Suctions

Skip the suction cup caddies. They fall off eventually, usually at 7am when you're mid-shampoo. A caddy that hangs from the shower head, like the mDesign version for about $20, holds way more weight and never budges.

## Baskets on Every Open Surface

This one feels almost too simple, but grouping loose items into a basket instantly makes a bathroom counter look intentional instead of chaotic. A $15 woven basket from Target's Threshold line can corral hair ties, extra soap, and cotton rounds in one spot. Loose bottles everywhere reads cluttered. The same bottles in a basket reads styled.

## Use the Back of the Door

Over-the-door organizers are not just for shoes. A clear vinyl pocket organizer meant for shoes works just as well on the back of a bathroom door for hair tools, sunscreen, and travel-size everything. It's ugly if left exposed, so this one is best for a door that closes and stays closed.

## Rolling Cart for Everything Else

A slim three-tier cart, like the Simple Trending rolling cart for around $35, is basically a mobile linen closet. It fits in the gap next to the toilet or the sink and rolls out when you need it. Renters love this one because it moves with you when the lease ends.

## Adhesive Hooks for Towels

Skip the towel bar installation entirely. Adhesive hooks rated for a few pounds, like Command's large size, hold a full bath towel without a problem. Put two at slightly different heights so towels can actually dry instead of overlapping into one damp pile.

## A Second Mirror With Storage Built In

If your medicine cabinet is tiny, an adhesive-mount mirror cabinet can go on an empty stretch of wall and add real storage without a drill. Many mount with the same heavy-duty strips used for towel hooks, rated for a surprising amount of weight.

## Store Backup Supplies in a Bin Elsewhere

Not everything needs to live in the bathroom. Extra toilet paper, backup shampoo, and the giant pack of cotton balls can live in a labeled bin in a hall closet or under the bed. Your bathroom only needs to hold what you use this week.

## A Tiered Tray on the Counter

A small tiered tray, the kind usually sold for jewelry, works great for skincare bottles on a counter with zero drawer space. It keeps things off the flat surface and makes wiping down the counter a ten-second job instead of a production.

## Magnetic Strips Inside the Cabinet Door

Nail clippers, tweezers, and bobby pins disappear into drawers forever. A magnetic strip stuck to the inside of a cabinet door keeps small metal tools visible and in one place. It's a five-dollar fix that solves a genuinely annoying problem.

## Corner Shelf in the Shower

If your shower has no built-in shelf, a tension-pole corner caddy sets up in minutes and holds shampoo, conditioner, and a razor off the floor. No suction cups, no drilling, and it comes down just as easily when you move.

The theme running through all of this is that small bathroom storage ideas do not need to be permanent to be effective. Rentals reward creativity more than commitment, and honestly, half of these solutions travel better than furniture does. Start with the over-the-toilet shelf and the command hooks. Those two alone will make the room feel twice as functional by next weekend.
